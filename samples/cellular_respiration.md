# Cellular Respiration and Metabolic Bioenergetics

## Module 1: Glycolysis in the Cytosol
Glycolysis is an anaerobic metabolic pathway that occurs in the cytosol of cells, converting one molecule of glucose (C6H12O6) into two molecules of pyruvate. It consumes 2 ATP in the preparatory energy-investment phase and produces 4 ATP via substrate-level phosphorylation and 2 NADH molecules, yielding a net gain of 2 ATP and 2 NADH per glucose.

## Module 2: The Transition Reaction and the Citric Acid (Krebs) Cycle
Pyruvate is actively transported into the mitochondrial matrix, where the Pyruvate Dehydrogenase complex converts each pyruvate into Acetyl-CoA, releasing CO2 and producing NADH. Acetyl-CoA then combines with oxaloacetate to form citrate in the Krebs cycle. Each turn of the cycle generates 3 NADH, 1 FADH2, 1 ATP (or GTP), and 2 CO2 molecules.

## Module 3: The Electron Transport Chain (ETC)
Located in the inner mitochondrial membrane, the electron transport chain consists of protein complexes (I, III, IV) and mobile electron carriers (ubiquinone and cytochrome c). High-energy electrons donated by NADH and FADH2 pass down the chain, driving the active pumping of protons (H+) from the matrix into the intermembrane space to generate a steep electrochemical proton gradient.

## Module 4: Chemiosmosis and ATP Synthase Mechanism
The proton-motive force drives protons down their electrochemical gradient back into the mitochondrial matrix through ATP Synthase (Complex V). The flow of protons induces rotational mechanical catalysis in the F1 unit of ATP Synthase, condensing ADP and inorganic phosphate into ATP. This oxidative phosphorylation generates approximately 26 to 28 ATP per glucose molecule.
